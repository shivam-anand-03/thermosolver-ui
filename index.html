Assemble the rows

Central differences inside, boundary rows from the conditions you set, shown as the augmented matrix [A | b].

Pivot and swap

Partial pivoting picks the largest entry. Zero and near-zero pivots are caught before they do damage.

Eliminate

Each row operation is recorded with its factor, so you can redo it by hand and get the same numbers.

Custom heat generation

Any heat source you can write. Typed like maths, solved like a matrix.

Write q‴ as a function of position in plain maths or LaTeX: sin(πx/L), a Gaussian hot spot, a ramp, a 2-D bump. The editor previews it live, plots it across the domain and catches mistakes before you solve.

Plain maths or LaTeX

5e5 sin(pi x / L) and \sin(\frac{\pi x}{L}) both work.

Safe on the server

An allow-list parser, never eval. Only maths functions and x, y, z, L, W, H.

FDM, FVM and FEM

Evaluated at every node, cell centre or element node, in 1-D, 2-D and 3-D.

Checked against the closed form

A sine source lands within 0.05 % of q₀L²/(kπ²) on 41 nodes, for all three methods.

One connected workflow

From problem statement to validated answer.

Three phases, eleven steps. A live diagram of the domain and the governing equation stay beside every step.

I
Phase I

Preprocessor

Describe the physics, the domain and the conditions on it.

  1. IProblem statementConduction, convection–diffusion or convection; steady or unsteady; 1-D to 3-D
  2. IIGeometry and gridL, W, H and Nx, Ny, Nz
  3. IIIPhysical phenomenaVelocity; Péclet, Fourier and Courant numbers
  4. IVMaterial propertiesk, ρ, cp for solids and fluids
  5. VInitial and boundary conditionsTemperature, flux, convective, insulated, inflow, outflow
II
Phase II

Solver

Choose how to discretise, how to invert, and when to stop.

  1. INumerical methodFDM with ghost nodes, FVM with upwind, central or hybrid faces, Galerkin FEM
  2. IIMatrix inversionGauss–Seidel and SOR, Gauss elimination, LU with PA = LU
  3. IIIConvergenceTolerance, iteration cap, ω, initial guess, θ-scheme
  4. IVRun calculationCtrl + Enter from any step
III
Phase III

Post-processing

Read the answer, then check it against something exact.

  1. IResultsNode table, fields, profiles, monitor history, [A] and its LU factors
  2. IIValidationExact solutions, error norms, residuals, grid convergence with the observed order

FAQ

Common questions.

Is CFD Solver free?
Yes. There is no account and nothing to install. Open the solver and start from a blank problem or one of the examples.
Can I use my own heat generation, such as sin(πx/L)?
Yes. In step I choose Custom f(x, y, z) and type the expression in plain maths or LaTeX, for example 5e5 sin(pi x / L). You can still add a βT term. The expression depends on position, not on time.
Why not just call numpy.linalg.solve?
Because the point is to see the elimination. Gaussian elimination, LU and Gauss–Seidel are written by hand so every pivot, swap and row operation can be recorded. The test suite compares them with NumPy.
Is Fo > 0.5 unstable?
Only for the explicit scheme. The fully implicit scheme is unconditionally stable for the diffusion equation, so CFD Solver never flags Fo > 0.5 there. It warns when a very large Fo (above 10) may hurt accuracy.
Which units can I use?
Lengths in m, cm or mm and temperatures in °C or K. Everything is SI internally, and the display unit can be switched in the results.
Is anything I enter stored?
Your set-up is sent to the API to be solved and is not kept there. The app saves your current set-up in your own browser so it survives a reload. The privacy policy has the details.
